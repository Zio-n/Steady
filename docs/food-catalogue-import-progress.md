## Quality review findings

- **Generic nutrient import repaired.** CoFID values were stored as text, and its importer had treated non-nutrient food factors as nutrients. It now parses numeric strings, records trace values separately, maps common nutrients, and excludes fatty-acid ratios measured per 100 g of fat. FNDDS nutrient IDs are matched by the source's nutrient number. CoFID has 2,853 foods with energy, 2,886 with protein, 1,547 with fibre, and 31 alcoholic drinks on the published per-100-ml basis.
- **USDA SR Legacy added for generic-food breadth.** Imported its final April 2018 release as a separate CC0 source: 7,793 foods, each with energy and protein, plus 14,430 verified portion rows across 7,533 foods. The archive is only 5.8 MB compressed. Nutrient values now retain source value/unit and a normalized-unit status; 636,332 SR Legacy nutrient rows are linked locally. This adds more generic forms and measurable portions, but the source is US-oriented and is no longer updated.
- **Units and implausible values cleaned for core nutrients.** Recognized Open Food Facts core units were converted to the catalogue unit. 4,679 common nutrient rows with unusable unit labels and 41,642 clearly out-of-range common values were removed. No core-unit mismatches or over-limit core values remain. Open Food Facts rows now marked incomplete when energy or protein is missing.
- **Open Food Facts unit enrichment completed locally.** All 12,322,169 OFF nutrient rows retain their imported value and unit, plus a row-level `value_unit` and normalization status. Of these, 10,769,450 are marked `normalized`, 130,691 remain `source_unit_only`, and 1,422,028 product scores/other attributes are marked `not_a_nutrient`. A total of 116,879 values are currently converted. A precision audit found positive trace values that would round to zero at the database's storage precision; those values were restored from their originals and left source-unit-only. Final QA found no normalized-unit mismatches, no changed source-only values, and no positive converted values rounded to zero.
- **Vitamin-specific conversions use explicit rules.** Vitamin A values already in IU remain in IU where that matches the nutrient definition; no IU-to-mass conversion is guessed because it depends on the form. Open Food Facts vitamin D now uses µg: FDA guidance states 1 IU equals 0.025 µg for vitamin D₂ and D₃. IU, µg, and mg rows are converted; gram-labelled, %DV, and precision-limited rows remain source-unit-only. [FDA conversion guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/129863/download).
- **Search and personal catalogue copies are connected locally.** The meal builder and calendar log/plan picker search the shared catalogue alongside private foods. Search uses indexed token-prefix matching across names, brands, and attributed aliases; it applies a browser-locale hint, prefers generic matches for unbranded searches, and returns source, basis, and normalized nutrients. A catalogue choice opens a correction step for name, nutrition basis, calories, and the user's nutrient fields. Saving creates a private food and source-origin link in one transaction. Later edits mark the private copy as personally adjusted; shared source rows remain unchanged. After the SR Legacy import, a common one-word query measured 102 ms locally.
- **Starter search sample is mixed.** A synthetic 30-query sample returned a candidate for 27 queries. All 24 generic/international queries returned candidates, while only 3 of 6 UK-branded queries did. It missed Alpro soya milk, Hovis seeded bread, and a chicken tikka ready meal. The Tesco Greek-yoghurt query's top result is a strawberry yoghurt, and Warburtons' top bread result is white rather than seeded. Candidate presence is not exact-match precision; some generic hits can be a cooked form. The sample is reproducible with `scripts/benchmark-catalogue-search.sql`, but it is not a substitute for real, frequency-weighted user searches.
- **Ambiguous units are kept out of calculations.** Rows with IU, %DV, volume percentages, unknown labels, or unsupported conversions retain the original amount and unit with `source_unit_only`. They remain available for source-aware display, but consumers must not combine them as normalized nutrient quantities. Unit conversion did not infer biological equivalence such as IU-to-mass.
- **Portions are uneven.** Local QA confirms portion data for 642,332 Open Food Facts products (642,333 gram portions), 5,395 FNDDS foods (22,045 gram portions), 291 Foundation Foods (298 gram portions), and 7,533 SR Legacy foods (14,430 household-portion rows). CoFID has no household portion records. Its 2021 workbook provides edible proportion for 2,852 distinct imported foods and specific gravity for 54 foods; these factors have been added to local source metadata and all food-code joins matched. They support edible-yield or food-specific g↔ml calculations only; they are not serving sizes. None of the imported portion records is currently marked as a default.
- **Priority generic variants reviewed and expanded.** CoFID, FNDDS, Foundation Foods, and SR Legacy contain source-backed raw/cooked, drained/canned, skin/bone, and fat-level forms across oats, rice, potatoes, chicken, beef, milk, eggs, legumes, pasta, and tomatoes. SR Legacy adds distinct forms and 7,533 foods with household portion conversions. This is a targeted family review, not proof of complete global variant coverage; search can still rank an inappropriate form too highly.
- **Allergen fields audited and separated locally.** Of 909,447 Open Food Facts products, 215,430 have source `allergens_tags` and 14,471 have `traces_tags`; 217,939 retain substantive ingredient/user evidence fields. The importer and local schema now keep possible traces separate from allergen tags, and preserve source evidence. CoFID, FNDDS, and Foundation imports provide no allergen fields. Missing or empty values remain unknown; none is treated as proof of allergen absence.
- **No duplicate barcodes found** within a source in the imported catalogue. One FNDDS item currently has no nutrient rows.
- **UK branded coverage is very low.** Only 14 imported Open Food Facts products retain the `en:united-kingdom` tag. CoFID supplies 2,886 UK generic foods, but the initial UK retailer-brand queries were mostly absent. The Open Food Facts import currently requires energy data, which excludes many tagged products.
- **Generic coverage is stronger than UK branded coverage.** The 30-query sample is a sanity check, not an at-scale gate. All 24 generic/international sample queries return candidates with calories and protein, but ranking can still select the wrong form: “chicken breast” ranked breaded tenders above the available plain/raw breast records. UK branded coverage is weak and some matches miss the exact variant. Search now works in both pickers; common-query latency was about 80 ms locally, but search quality still needs a larger, frequency-weighted review.
- **A proposed Open Food Facts filter relaxation was rejected after measurement.** The full archive scan found only 3,015 named products with core macros but no energy, versus 909,458 named products with energy; none of those 3,015 carries the UK country tag. They would be incomplete for meal logging and would require users to supply calories, so the local catalogue was not refreshed with them.

## Overall assessment

The current local catalogue is large and global, and the generic nutrient import is now substantially healthier. It is **not yet sufficient to claim users will rarely need Add Food**: UK branded products are sparse, generic top matches can use the wrong preparation, CoFID lacks household portions, and some micronutrients remain source-unit-only. The 500-item, frequency-weighted coverage benchmark has not been run because real user food searches are not available in the project.

## Import tooling and provenance

- Schema: `supabase/migrations/20261030120000_food_catalogue.sql`.
- Importers: `scripts/import-food-catalogue.py` (CoFID), `scripts/import-usda-foods.py` (FNDDS/Foundation/SR Legacy), and `scripts/import-openfoodfacts.py` (global Open Food Facts).
- Open Food Facts source archive: `/Users/zion/TemporaryDownloads/openfoodfacts-products.jsonl.gz`; streamed directly without extraction. The source provides 4,884,059 product rows; the catalogue retains rows that pass the importer’s name and energy-data filters. A full scan confirmed relaxing that energy filter would add only 3,015 macro-only non-UK products, so the filter remains.
- USDA SR Legacy archive: official April 2018 FoodData Central CSV download (5.8 MB compressed); imported to local Supabase. No source dump is stored in the repository. USDA states its FoodData Central data are public domain/CC0 and requests source credit.
- Open Food Facts records remain separate by `source_id`; source/licence details are stored in `catalog_sources`. The source is ODbL 1.0. The hosted database has not been updated.

## Recommended next work

1. Improve Open Food Facts UK coverage and retain useful partial-nutrient products as `incomplete`; measure exact and acceptable product variants separately.
2. Review the 130,691 source-unit-only OFF rows by nutrient and unit; add only evidence-backed conversion rules before exposing them in calculations.
3. Make verified source portions available as selectable measures. Add CoFID household portions only if a credible source provides them; keep edible yield, density conversion, and household portions distinct.
4. Gather or author a frequency-weighted set of at least 500 realistic food searches and measure exact/acceptable match, usable nutrition, and portion availability separately. Keep the synthetic sample as a smoke check only.
